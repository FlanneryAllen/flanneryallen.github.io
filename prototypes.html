Prototypes • AI Systems & Narrative Intelligence

Can storytelling instincts become software?

Exploring whether human judgment can become a system without removing the human from the judgment.

After years of finding the story inside complicated things, I started wondering whether the instinct itself could be modeled. Not the writing. The judgment that comes before it.

01 Approach

First, I broke the instinct apart.

Storytelling can feel intuitive. But look closely and there are decisions happening underneath it.

Three of them kept showing up.

What matters? What's missing? What's changing? And what story is trying to emerge?

So I started building.

02 Tool 1

What matters?

Matter Meter

Not everything deserves the same weight.

Matter Meter was an experiment in separating signal from noise: looking at information in context and identifying what actually matters to the story.

Matter Meter
03 Tool 2

What's missing?

Story Gap Mapper

Sometimes the most important part of a story is the part that isn't there.

Story Gap Mapper looked across an existing narrative for holes: unanswered questions, unsupported claims, missing context, and places where the story didn't quite connect.

Story Gap Mapper
04 Tool 3

What's emerging?

Story Signal

Stories don't stay still.

Story Signal explored whether patterns across information could reveal what's changing, what's becoming important, and where the next story might be forming.

Story Signal
05 Integration

Then I put the instincts together.

Narrative Agent

Matter. Gaps. Signals.

Individually, each tool answered one question.

But storytellers don't think one question at a time.

We move between them constantly, weighing what matters against what's missing, connecting new information to what we already know, and adjusting the story as the world changes around it.

What if a system could do that too?

Narrative Agent brings those ideas together.

It reads across an organization's story—not just a single document—looking for the relationships between what the organization says, what it knows, what's changed, and what's missing.

It can surface narrative gaps, contradictions, emerging signals, and places where the story no longer adds up.

I think of it as narrative algebra.

Not AI that writes the story for you.

AI that helps you see the story more clearly.

Narrative Agent
06 Impact

Why it matters

Organizations don't have a shortage of content.

They have websites, decks, research, executive talking points, product messaging, employee communications, customer stories, strategy documents, transcripts, launches, and thousands of other fragments.

The problem is seeing the story across all of it.

The Questions That Matter

Where are we aligned?

Where are we contradicting ourselves?

What are we saying without proving?

What do we know that we're not saying?

What changed?

And what should the story become next?

That's the problem Narrative Agent is designed to explore.

07 Future

The bigger experiment

Narrative Agent started as a question about storytelling.

It became an experiment in something larger:

Can human judgment become a system without removing the human from the judgment?

I don't think the interesting future of AI and communications is machines generating more content.

We already have plenty of content.

The interesting part is whether AI can help us understand what all that information means, recognize what doesn't fit, and make better decisions about the story we tell next.

That's the part I'm still exploring.
